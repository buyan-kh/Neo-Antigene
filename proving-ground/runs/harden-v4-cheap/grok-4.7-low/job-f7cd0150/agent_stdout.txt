The benchmark is contaminated. **514** of the 1,486 test pairs are also in the records the predictor was fit on, so the published score is not trustworthy.

Those pairs sit in the three training panels (`panel_a.csv`, `panel_b.tsv`, and `panel_c.csv`). Matching them required normalizing allele names (`HLA-A*02:01`, `A*0201`, `A0201`, four-field IDs, and the `allele_id` map) and taking the uppercase core peptide out of the flanked sequences in `panel_b.tsv`. The 80 pairs listed in `held_out_from_fit.csv` are in the panels but were excluded from the fit, so they are not counted as leaked. The same peptide–allele pair never shows up in more than one panel.

On the leaked pairs the predictor’s AUROC is 1.0. On the **972** test pairs that were not used to build it, the AUROC is **0.780**.

The result is in `output/submission.json` (`mechanism`: `train_test_overlap`).
