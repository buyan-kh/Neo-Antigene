# The study

## The job

A tumor has thousands of possible pieces. A vaccine can carry about twenty to
thirty-four of them. The job is to choose those pieces for one person, from
that person's own mutations and HLA, and to know whether the choice was better
than the usual computer guess.

The first study does not inject anyone. A hospital consents the patient and
connects that person's blood to that person's tumor. A contract lab makes the
pieces and runs the immune-cell test. The lab sends back yes or no. The lab
does not own the result. The contract-lab step is about twenty-five thousand
dollars a person. The hospital introduction is the part we cannot buy.

## What "honest" means

Each piece comes from a real mutation in that tumor. The person's HLA can
display it. The picking rule is locked before the blood result comes back.
The computer score is not a yes. The same blood scores the frozen list and
the usual sort. The size of the study is stated first. These patients are
not used to retrain the rule and then quoted as the win.

Training on the same people you score is the mistake already visible in
published tools. See [field-audit.md](field-audit.md).

## What forty people can show

A gap of about twice as many immune yeses as the usual presentation sort can
show up in about forty people, at about twenty peptides each. That is on the
order of a hundred yeses in total. A smaller gap means this study cannot
tell. That is a result, and it should be said.

Two public cases are too small to be that study:

- Ott and colleagues, melanoma, 2017. A list of twenty needs five yeses
  before it clears a simple chance bar. The published lists are shorter than
  a full menu of mutations. The hard selection already happened before we
  rank them.
- One lung-cancer sample (CU04) has nineteen peptides and three yeses. No
  ranking of that pool can reach a conventional significance line, even if
  all three yeses land at the top. The best possible result on that pool is
  still compatible with chance. `docs/BENCHMARK.md` has the run.

The first forty grade the rule we walk in with. A later group is the first
place a new rule can be trained, then tested on people it has never seen.

## What a computer can grade

A computer can check things that have a markable answer: whether a peptide
can be derived from a mutation and a proteome, whether two allele spellings
are the same gene, whether a published test set was already in the training
files, and whether a study is large enough to see a gap.

A computer cannot mark a T-cell yes. That mark is the blood test. Agent
swarms belong on the first kind of question. They do not replace the study.

## How this becomes a business

Patients do not pay. The study is what a raise is for. If the locked list is
clearly better than the usual guess, a company that already makes these
vaccines can pay to use the list. If the study cannot tell the two lists
apart, there is nothing to license yet.

The thing being licensed is the picking rule, after it has been graded. It is
not the patient answer file, and it is not a factory. Manufacturing stays
with a company that already does it.
