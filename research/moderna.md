# Moderna's program

Moderna and Merck's individualized shot is intismeran autogene, also called
V940 and mRNA-4157. It is an mRNA vaccine in a lipid particle. It carries up
to 34 bits from that person's tumor, and it is given with pembrolizumab
(Keytruda) after the tumor has been removed.

The inputs they have described are tumor whole-exome sequencing, matched
normal whole-exome sequencing, tumor RNA sequencing, and HLA typed from
sequencing. The weights of the picker are private. A patent
(US20210268086A1) describes embodiments, including window size, NetMHCpan,
expression, and clonality. An embodiment is not the production recipe.
US12331361 is a Personal Genome Diagnostics patent. It is not Moderna's.

## What the trials reported

**KEYNOTE-942**, phase 2b, melanoma after surgery, vaccine plus Keytruda
versus Keytruda alone. Lancet, Weber and colleagues, 2024. The primary
result was a hazard ratio of about 0.561 for recurrence or death. The ASCO 2026 follow-up was about a 49% reduction in
recurrence or death (hazard ratio 0.51) and about a 59% reduction in distant
metastasis or death (hazard ratio 0.411). In that study, 91% of combination
patients received 34 antigens (median 34, range 9–34).

**INTerpath-001** (NCT05933577). Company topline, 19 August 2026. 1,137
patients. The study met its primary endpoint, recurrence-free survival, and
a key secondary endpoint, distant metastasis-free survival, against Keytruda
alone. The companies called it the first positive phase 3 for an
individualized neoantigen therapy and for an mRNA cancer therapy. The numeric
phase 3 results were not in that topline. Full results were scheduled for
the ESMO 2026 presidential symposium on 24 October 2026. Overall survival
was still being followed. Safety was described as consistent, with no new
signals.

This is evidence that the category can work in melanoma after the tumor is
cut out, given with Keytruda. It is not a cure, and it is not evidence about
every cancer.

## The colon score we cannot rerun

AACR 2020 abstract 6539 (Zhong, Breton, Zheng, and colleagues). The same
algorithm used for mRNA-4157 was run on sequencing from 29 people with
microsatellite-stable colorectal cancer, shared by the National Cancer
Institute. Immune cells from those tumors had already recognized 64 bits.
The vaccine design included 26 of those 64 (41%). At least one recognized
bit was included for 18 of 29 people (61%). All of a person's recognized
bits were included for 8 of 29 (28%).

They never published which 64, or which 26. The 64 are an unnamed subset of
the colorectal hits in Parkhurst and colleagues, 2019. See
[public-exams.md](public-exams.md). Beating "26 of 64" requires the same 64
and the same match rule, with the rule frozen first. That exam cannot be
run from public data.

## The list they did publish

Gainor and colleagues, *Cancer Discovery* 2024, KEYNOTE-603
(NCT03313778). DOI 10.1158/2159-8290.CD-24-0158. The peptide list and HLA
types are on Figshare, article 32699640, file
`cd-24-0158_peptide_list_and_hla_types_suppst.xlsx`. Counted from that file
on 8 October 2026.

The study is the first-in-human one: 4 people with non-small-cell lung
cancer after surgery, vaccine alone, and 12 people with melanoma after
surgery, vaccine plus pembrolizumab.

The spreadsheet has two sheets.

**Vaccine rows.** 360 pieces across 11 people. Ten people have 34 pieces.
One person (patient 4) has 20. Columns are patient id, gene, protein change,
whether the piece was included as class I, class II, or both, a long
sequence (317 of the 360 are 25 letters), the matching normal sequence, one
short piece, a pool, and an id. The class marks are 305 class I only, 22
class II only, and 33 both. Every row has a long sequence and a short
piece. Most short pieces are 8 to 11 letters.

**HLA.** 16 people, which is the whole small study. Each person has rows for
A, B, C, and the class II genes. Five of the 16 have HLA and no peptide
list in the file.

There is no column that says the blood answered yes or no. A meeting
abstract (SITC 2023, abstract 1530) says T-cell testing was available for 3
of 4 people in the lung cohort and 7 of 12 in the melanoma cohort, and that
neoantigen-specific T cells were induced in all 10 of those people. The
paper says the same thing in words: new responses and strengthened existing
ones. Neither the paper's tables nor this spreadsheet say which of the 34
pieces the blood liked.

So the file shows the shape of a finished recipe: one person, their HLA, and
up to 34 chosen bits, mostly a long stretch plus one short piece, covering
both killer and helper T cells. It does not show the mutations they threw
away. It does not grade the choice. It is not the colon score sheet.

A 2019 Moderna investor exhibit described one earlier patient with CD8
responses to 10 of 18 class I pieces, and said those positives had predicted
binding under 500 nM. That is one person. It is not a recipe.
