What we do

The vaccine is real. The list is still a guess.

A personal cancer vaccine holds a few dozen bits of one person’s tumor. A computer chooses the bits. We check that choice on the patient’s own blood.

A tumor has thousands of possible bits.

What changed in August

Moderna and Merck reported that a vaccine built from a patient’s own tumor, given with Keytruda after the melanoma was removed, kept the cancer away longer than Keytruda alone.

August 2026

First time this kind of vaccine has won a large trial. The size of the benefit is not public yet. Full numbers are expected later this month. Survival is still being counted.

  • Keytruda is a drug that lets immune cells keep going. The vaccine names the target. Keytruda lets the response run.
  • The patients had melanoma that had been fully cut out. The goal was to stop it coming back.
  • The shot is different for every person. The COVID vaccine is one recipe for everyone. This one is written from that tumor.

Their trial left one question open

The package works. The trial was not built to ask whether a better choice of bits would have kept more people well.

We lock the list, then ask the blood

For each patient we choose about twenty bits and freeze that choice before anyone sees a result. A lab makes the bits and mixes them with a sample of that person’s blood. Each bit comes back yes or no.

Makes the bits and runs the immune-cell test. About twenty-five thousand dollars a person. They send back yes or no. They do not own the result.

Finer points, still in plain words

One person does not transfer. The bits that work for one tumor are usually absent from the next person. A rule learned across many people can be reused. A list copied from one patient cannot.
Their recipe is private. We know Moderna’s inputs and that the cap is 34 bits. We do not have the weights. On 29 colon cancers, immune cells had already recognized 64 bits. Their list included 26 of those 64. They never published which 26, so that score cannot be copied.
A public paper names 94 colon hits, as a gene and the letter that changed, across 51 patients. That is a larger, related list. It is not Moderna’s score sheet, and it is not enough, by itself, to crown a better program.
We have not shown our list is better. The study is how we find out. A clean “we cannot tell” is a result we can stand behind.
